<h1 id="kerfuffle">Kerfuffle</h1>
<p>Kerfuffle is a tactical card game for two players. Each player commands an army of creatures and buildings drawn from four elemental factions — Bomb, Corn, Goop, and Storm — and deploys them across a shared battlefield to wage war over a series of fortified cities and outposts.</p>
<p>Victory goes to the player who destroys two of their opponent’s outposts and captures the majority of their opponent’s cities. Every action matters: where you move, what you attack, and when you strike can mean the difference between a crumbling defense and a dramatic comeback.</p>
<h2 id="terminology">Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Asset</strong> — Anything that can be targeted in an attack: creatures, buildings, infrastructure tokens, cities, and outposts.</li>
<li><strong>Counter</strong> — A piece placed on a card to track health, status effects, elemental power, or action availability.</li>
<li><strong>Territory</strong> — The rows within each player’s half of the board, as represented by the color of squares.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="setup">Setup</h2>
<ol>
<li>The board is a 11x6 grid divided evenly so each player controls three rows.</li>
<li>Place stacks of three city and outpost tiles on each row according to the default setup positions.</li>
<li>Shuffle the creature and building decks separately. Deal each player <strong>three creature cards</strong>, and <strong>three building cards</strong>.</li>
<li>Blue places two creatures following normal placement rules.</li>
<li>Red takes the first turn.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="round-structure">Round Structure</h2>
<p>Each turn consists of five phases, always performed in this order:</p>
<h3 id="passive-effects">1. Passive Effects</h3>
<ul>
<li>All friendly creatures that are <strong>Aflame</strong> roll a Barrage die and take damage accordingly.</li>
<li>All friendly corn creatures on a square with a corn field gain the <strong>Buttered Up</strong> status effect.</li>
<li>All friendly creatures on a square with a storm cell gain the <strong>Sodden</strong> status effect.</li>
<li>Flip all action counters to the green <em>action available</em> side.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="card-placement">2. Card Placement</h3>
<p>Play up to two cards from your hand.</p>
<p>To play a card, place it face-up it into your tableau. Place its standee on a <strong>valid</strong> square.</p>
<ul>
<li>Creatures can only be placed on a friendly outpost or <strong>non-besieged</strong> friendly city.</li>
<li>Buildings can be played anywhere within your territory.</li>
</ul>
<p>Place a health tracker displaying the asset’s <strong>max HP</strong> in the top-right corner of the card . Place an action counter showing the green <em>action available</em> side on the bottom-right corner of the card.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Validity Rules:</strong> Newly placed building and creatures must obey standard occupancy rules. See <em>Square Occupancy</em>.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Card Maximum:</strong> Each player may have a maximum of four creatures and two buildings in play at any time. You may not play a card if it would exceed this limit.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>After card placement, you may choose to retire one building or creature by removing its standee from the board and discarding its card.</p>
<h3 id="building-actions">3. Building Actions</h3>
<p>For each building in your tableau, perform both of the following, then flip its action counter to the grey <em>action used</em> side.</p>
<ol>
<li>Place a power token on a friendly creature that shares the building’s elemental type.</li>
<li>May use the building’s special ability as described on its card.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="creature-actions">4. Creature Actions</h3>
<p>For each creature in your tableau, perform one of the following actions, then flip its action counter to the grey <em>action used</em> side.</p>
<h4 id="move">Move</h4>
<p>Move the creature to any square permitted by its <strong>movement pattern</strong>. Creatures may move into enemy territory.</p>
<ul>
<li>Orientation matters. You may rotate the creature either before or after moving, but <strong>not both in the same action</strong>.</li>
<li>You may use a move action to <strong>rotate only</strong>, without moving to a different square.</li>
<li>Moving onto a <strong>besieged</strong> enemy city captures it. See <em>Sieging Cities</em>.</li>
</ul>
<h4 id="attack--heal">Attack / Heal</h4>
<ol>
<li>Choose one asset within the creature’s attack pattern as the target.</li>
<li>Roll the number of dice shown for the target’s square from the creature’s dice pool.</li>
<li>Apply the result based on the target type:
<ul>
<li><strong>Creature or Building</strong>: Deal damage equal to the number of hit icons shown on the dice. If the attacker or target is reduced to 0HP, remove it from the board and discard its card.</li>
<li><strong>Infrastructure Token</strong>: Any hits destroy the token.</li>
<li><strong>City or Outpost</strong>: Any hits remove one tile from its stack.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If the target was a creature, the attacker takes retaliatory damage equal to the number of circled hit icons shown.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Healing:</strong> A creature with blue dice in its pool may target a creature to heal instead of dealing damage. The target gains 1HP for each icon shown on the blue dice.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> The square labeled with the pawprint icon is considered to be part of a creature’s attack range with a value of one. A creature may use one die to attack an infrastructure token, city, or outpost on its own square. A creature may heal itself using one die.</p>
</blockquote>
<h4 id="use-ability">Use Ability</h4>
<p>If the creature card has a <strong>power counter</strong>, perform the special ability as described on the card, then remove the counter.</p>
<h3 id="discard--draw">5. Discard &amp; Draw</h3>
<p>Discard up to two cards to their respective discard piles. Then draw from the creature deck, building deck, or any combination until you have <strong>six cards</strong> in hand. If either deck is empty, shuffle its discard pile to form a new deck.</p>
<h2 id="sieging-cities">Sieging Cities</h2>
<p>Cities are represented by a stack of tiles representing siege defenses.</p>
<p>Each time a city is hit in an attack, <strong>one</strong> tile is removed from its stack. A city reduced to one tile is considered <strong>besieged</strong> and cannot be reduced further. Creatures cannot be played onto a besieged city.</p>
<p>A city is captured when a creature moves onto a besieged enemy city. When this happens, flip the remaining tile to your color, and add one tile to the stack for a total of two.</p>
<h2 id="outposts">Outposts</h2>
<p>Like cities, outposts are represented by a stack of defense tiles. Each time an outpost is hit in an attack,  <strong>one</strong> tile is removed from its stack. When every tile in the outpost is removed, the outpost is destroyed.</p>
<p>Creatures can always be placed on outposts regardless of the number of tiles in its stack. Outposts <strong>reduce incoming hits</strong> by one for any friendly creature occupying them. This applies to both direct and retaliatory damage.</p>
<h2 id="winning-the-game">Winning the Game</h2>
<p>A player wins the game when they fulfill one of the following requirements:</p>
<ol>
<li>Hold at least three cities AND have destroyed two enemy outposts.</li>
<li>Hold all cities.</li>
<li>Remove all enemy deployment squares.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="cards">Cards</h2>
<h3 id="creature-cards">Creature Cards</h3>
<p>Creatures are the core of the game. They can move, attack, and capture cities. They can also push deep into enemy territory to destroy buildings and disrupt infrastructure.</p>
<p>Each creature card includes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Element Type</strong> — The creature’s elemental affiliation.</li>
<li><strong>Maximum Health</strong> — The creature’s starting HP. It is destroyed when reduced to 0 and may never exceed this value.</li>
<li><strong>Attack Pattern</strong> — A grid showing which squares the creature can target relative to its position and orientation. Numbers indicate how many dice from its pool may be used for each square.</li>
<li><strong>Dice Pool</strong> — The colors and quantities of dice available for attacks and healing.</li>
<li><strong>Movement Pattern</strong> — A grid showing which squares the creature can move to in a single move action.</li>
<li><strong>Special Ability</strong> — An action usable only when the creature has a power counter.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Creature Placement:</strong> Creatures may only be placed on a friendly outpost or non-besieged friendly city. See <em>Sieging Cities</em>.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="building-cards">Building Cards</h3>
<p>Buildings power creatures abilities and provide passive effects each turn. Unlike creatures, buildings cannot move.</p>
<p>Each building card includes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Element Type</strong> — The building’s elemental affiliation. It can only power creatures of the same type.</li>
<li><strong>Maximum Health</strong> — The building’s starting HP. It is destroyed when reduced to 0 and may never exceed this value.</li>
<li><strong>Range Pattern</strong> — A grid showing which squares the building can target when using its passive ability.</li>
<li><strong>Passive Ability</strong> — An action taken each turn during the <em>Building Actions</em> phase.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Building Placement:</strong> Buildings may only be placed on unoccupied squares within your territory. See <em>Square Occupancy</em>.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="infrastructure-tokens">Infrastructure Tokens</h2>
<p>Infrastructure tokens are assets placed directly on the board with no corresponding card. They are generated by certain creature and building abilities and can apply status effects or enable special interactions.</p>
<p>Tokens have no sense of ownership once placed. They may benefit or inhibit all players equally.</p>
<p>Tokens are destroyed upon taking any damage.</p>
<h2 id="square-occupancy">Square Occupancy</h2>
<ul>
<li>Each square may hold <strong>one creature or building</strong>, but not both.</li>
<li>A creature and a token <strong>may</strong> coexist on a square.</li>
<li>A building and a token <strong>may not</strong> coexist on a square.</li>
<li>Two tokens <strong>may not</strong> coexist on a square.</li>
<li>Cities and outposts follow the same occupancy rules as tokens.</li>
</ul>

</table><h2 id="status-effects">Status Effects</h2>
<p>Status effects are applied through creature abilities, building abilities, and infrastructure token interactions. They are tracked with counters placed at the top of the card.</p>
<ul>
<li>When a <strong>debuffed</strong> asset is healed, one point of healing is spent removing the debuff before restoring HP.</li>
<li>When a <strong>buffed</strong> asset is attacked, one hit is spent removing the buff before dealing damage.</li>
<li>Status effects <strong>cannot</strong> be applied to infrastructure tokens, cities or outposts.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="elemental-types">Elemental Types</h2>
<p>Every asset belongs to one of four elemental types. Each element has four creatures, two buildings, a unique elemental ability, an associated status effect, and an infrastructure token.</p>
<hr>
<h3 id="bomb">Bomb</h3>
<p><strong>Elemental Ability:</strong> Bomb creatures explode when reduced to 0 HP, or <strong>immediately</strong> when set Aflame. On explosion, roll one Barrage die.  All assets on the bomb’s square and each surrounding square — including friendly assets — take the damage shown.</p>
<p><strong>Status Effect (<em>Debuff</em>) — Aflame:</strong> During the <em>Passive Effects</em> phase, the affected asset rolls a Barrage die and takes damage accordingly.</p>
<p><strong>Infrastructure Token — Bomb:</strong> Bomb tokens explode when destroyed.</p>
<hr>
<h3 id="corn">Corn</h3>
<p><strong>Elemental Ability:</strong> During the <em>Passive Effects</em> phase, friendly corn creatures occupying a corn field gain the <strong>Buttered Up</strong> status effect.</p>
<p><strong>Status Effect (<em>Buff</em>) — Buttered Up:</strong> The creature may reroll any one die when attacking or healing.</p>
<p><strong>Infrastructure Token — Corn Field:</strong> In addition to enabling the corn elemental ability, corn fields reduce incoming hits by two for any creatures occupying them. This applies to both direct and retaliatory damage.</p>
<hr>
<h3 id="goop">Goop</h3>
<p><strong>Elemental Ability:</strong> When a Goop creature deals retaliatory damage, the attacker gains the <strong>Slimy</strong> status effect. When a Goop creature moves, it leaves an ooze token on the square it vacated. Goop creatures are not affected by Slimy or Ooze.</p>
<p><strong>Status Effect (<em>Debuff</em>) — Slimy:</strong> Slimy creatures cannot be powered by buildings. If a creature is already powered when Slimy is applied, it may still use its ability. Slimy buildings cannot be used to power creatures.</p>
<p><strong>Infrastructure Token — Ooze:</strong> A creature occupying a square with ooze token cannot move. Movement is only allowed once the ooze token is destroyed, or when pushed off the ooze by a storm creature.</p>
<hr>
<h3 id="storm">Storm</h3>
<p><strong>Elemental Ability:</strong> When a Storm creature deals (non-retaliatory) damage to a non-storm enemy creature, it must immediately move that creature one square in any direction, including diagonally. Do not rotate the move creature. This does not affect buildings or tokens.</p>
<p><strong>Status Effect (<em>Debuff</em>) — Sodden:</strong> The affected creature rolls one fewer die on all rolls. If this causes a creature to have zero attack dice, it cannot attack. Aflame is immediately removed when Sodden is applied.</p>
<p><strong>Infrastructure Token — Storm Cells:</strong> During the <em>Passive Effects</em> phase, friendly creatures occupying a storm cell gain the Sodden status effect. Storm cells are also referenced by various storm card abilities.</p>
